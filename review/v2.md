# Review of Version 2: «مغامرة المستكشف علي» (custom WebGL2 mini-engine)

Source: `ref/v2.html` (2,182 lines, 162 KB, no external JS; one Google Font). Line numbers below refer to that file.
I read the whole file and ran it headless (Chromium + SwiftShader, 1280×800 plus portrait and phone viewports).
Scripts: `tools/v2_shots.mjs` (full flow), `tools/v2_shots2.mjs` (clean post-fade shots, scene statistics, results animation, tablet and phone), `tools/v2_shots3.mjs` (checks of Ali's feet and the earthquake end state).
Screenshots are in `shots/`, named `v2_*.png` (index at the end).

---

## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

V2 is a calm, comfortable, well-paced and coherent VR lesson. Its strongest parts are:
- the VR plumbing (rig that keeps the head on the pad, snap turn around the head, controller ray and gaze-dwell, fade transitions);
- a single consistent pastel art direction;
- crisp Arabic canvas text with mipmaps;
- a very good **erosion** station;
- quiz thumbnails rendered from the real 3D stations.

It is let down by four things:
- **Placement bugs.** Several things that should be seen sit below other surfaces: 3 of the 5 earthquake cracks, the weathering ring, every contact shadow at the stations, Ali's feet in the hub and the hall, and the erosion "من هنا" sign, which hides behind the station board.
- **Lighting on UI.** The UI materials receive world lighting, so quiz cards turn grey in the challenge hall.
- **Shallow product.** There is no hands-on interaction, no haptics, no per-station progress, no teacher tools, the narration is text only on Quest, and the font is fetched from the network.
- **Heavy renderer for Quest 2.** About 589k triangles and about 396 draw calls per eye, drawn twice, with no culling, multiview or foveation.

The engine is clean but naive. For the new game, keep V2's interaction and comfort design and its content structure, and render with three.js (as V1 does).

## 2. Component quality assessment

### 2.1 Engine (M3, lines 124-738)
- **Math and scene graph.** A small, correct mat4 library: YXZ Euler compose (175-187), inverse, a normal matrix per draw (211-219). The `Node` graph supports `matrixOverride` (used for XR poses) and `shown()`. Fine.
- **Geometry kit.** plane, roundedBox (true rounded normals), sphere, cylinder, cone, disc, ring (ordered so `drawCount` can sweep it as a progress ring), torus, lathe, tube (parallel-transport frames, radiusFn, `segIndexCount` for grow-along-path), star, ico with jitter, flat-shade, and a merge that bakes vertex colours (289-534). This is the most reusable part. `drawCount`-based "grow" animation (cracks, lava, dwell ring) is an elegant trick.
- **Shading (545-575).** A single uber-shader: hemisphere ambient plus a two-step *banded* diffuse (smoothstep 0.7/0.3; a soft toon look), rim light, emissive, distance fog, premultiplied alpha. It reads as a pleasant pastel claymation. There is **no specular, no shadow map, no AO and no sRGB/tone mapping**. Lighting is computed on gamma-space colours.
- **Culling.** `Material.doubleSided` exists (281) but the renderer always disables `CULL_FACE` (679). Every closed mesh rasterises its back faces, and `fixWinding` (444-453) is needed only to make two-sided lighting correct.
- **Transparency.** Anything with a `map` is transparent and does not write depth (275, 280). Transparent meshes are sorted by `renderOrder` first and only then by distance from the object origin (684-685). As a result, a far object with a higher `renderOrder` paints over a nearer one. This is visible: Ali's bubble (renderOrder 3, 1222) is 3.5 m away yet cuts through the nearer "سؤال تدريبي" tag (renderOrder 1, 2.1 m) in `v2_practice.png`.
- **Textures.** Canvas uploads use premultiplied alpha, flip-Y, mipmaps and 4× anisotropy (619-634), which suits VR legibility. Render targets have a depth renderbuffer but **no MSAA** (635-647), so thumbnails are aliased.
- **Resource management.** There is none: no `deleteTexture`, `deleteBuffer` or `deleteVertexArray` anywhere. Each quiz question builds new Cards (each with 2 canvases) and a new picture plane (1903-1906). Retries leak GPU objects slowly.
- **Raycasting (713-735).** The ray is moved into each mesh's local space and tested against the geometry's AABB, so it is effectively an OBB test. That is exact enough for the box-shaped buttons and cards, which are the only targets (`UI.targets`). Limitations:
  - no triangle-level picking, so scene objects cannot be interacted with;
  - no occlusion by non-target geometry, so a ray can press a button through a board or through Ali;
  - the nearest hit is chosen before the `enabled` check (1983, 2029), so a disabled nearer target blocks an enabled one behind it.
- **Fade.** A full-screen triangle drawn per eye after the scene, in warm white (687-690). Comfortable.

### 2.2 Environment (1092-1142)
- **Island and sky.** A circular island (sand and grass cylinders) in a 250 m sea disc. The sky is a vertex-coloured gradient dome (horizon peach, then pale blue, then blue). There are 40 grass patches, a path ring at r = 15 linking the stations, 26 low-poly hills on a far ring, about 70 trees and palms placed with a view-corridor exclusion test per station (1107-1114, 1125), 5 palms framing the hub, 90 bush clusters with flowers, 30 rocks and a hub plaza.
- **Draw calls.** Everything is merged into **one draw call** with vertex colours (1131). That is a good batching idea, but the mesh has **447,884 triangles**, mostly 16×12 spheres in tree canopies and bushes and 5-vertex palm leaflets. It is drawn in full, twice per frame in VR.
- **Clouds.** 14 merged puff clusters slowly rotating (1134-1140).
- **Look.** A cohesive, cheerful pastel garden (`v2_hub.png`, `v2_hub_ali_feet.png`). It looks generic and green rather than Bahraini: there is no sea or coast vignette near the stations, no desert or sabkha, and no local landmarks.

### 2.3 Date palms and trees
- **`palmItems` (1026-1074)** is the best natural asset in either file:
  - curved ringed trunk (tube with a ring modulation `sin(u·π·22)`);
  - crown bulb;
  - 16 fronds in 3 elevation layers with droop, 18 leaflet pairs each (pinnate, colour-jittered), plus 3 dry hanging fronds;
  - 3 hanging date clusters of 16 dates each.

  It is clearly a *date palm*, which is culturally right for Bahrain. Cost is about 6k triangles per palm.
- **`treeItems` (1075-1089)**: a tapered trunk, 3 branches and 9 canopy spheres. Cute, but about 3.5k triangles each, so it is the main source of the triangle count.

### 2.4 Ali (1159-1258)
- **Proportions.** A deliberately *natural* 9-year-old about 1.32 m tall with the head about 1/6 of the height (comment at 1160). He reads as a real boy, but his face is small at the 3-3.5 m he stands from the pad, and he looks a little lanky. A child-friendly mascot usually needs a head of 1/4-1/5 for expressiveness.
- **Outfit.** White thobe made by lathe with a placket and buttons, a khaki explorer vest with pockets, a bush hat with a teal band, a backpack with a Bahrain-flag patch (accurate 5-point serration, 1153-1158; it is on his back, so the viewer never sees it), and sandals. Culturally appropriate and well observed.
- **Gestures.**
  - Available: idle sway, breathing scale, head drift and random blinks, plus wave, point, cheer, talk and encourage, each blended with ramp factors (1239-1258).
  - Mouth: a fixed torus smile (1203). There is no lip-sync and no sad or thinking expression, even when he says "لا بأس".
  - Arms: plain cylinders, and hands are ellipsoid blobs with a stub thumb.
  - Cheer: mid-motion it looks like a T-pose (`v2_st1_after.png`).
- **Placement bug.** `placeAli` sets y = 0 for the hub and the hall (1783), but the hub plaza top is y = 0.07 (1130) and the hall floor top is y = 0.12 (1720). His feet and sandals are **buried** and the thobe is cut flat (`v2_hub_ali_feet.png`, `v2_quiz.png`).

### 2.5 Speech bubble (1222, 1226-1238, 1257)
- **Build.** A 1.38×0.58 m panel at 560 px/m, with a drop shadow, a white rounded box, a teal stroke and a tail. Text is up to 3 lines and shrinks to fit (62 px start). It pops in with backOut.
- **Readability.** Very crisp and readable in every screenshot. On Quest, with no Arabic TTS, it carries the whole narrative.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Long feedback lines shrink.
  - The tail points below the bubble's centre, not at Ali's mouth.
  - It is not billboarded (fine from the pad).
  - The `renderOrder` 3 problem above.
  - There is no "repeat line" control.

### 2.6 Buttons, cards and panels (794-928)
- **Panel.** A rounded-box frame plus an unlit canvas face 3 mm in front, at 400-560 px/m. Text is shaped correctly (Chromium canvas RTL), with good weights (Baloo Bhaijaan 2).
- **Button.**
  - Body: a rounded box with a canvas label (icon placed on the RTL leading side).
  - Feedback: on hover it scales by 1.07, moves +2 cm and gains emissive; on press it shrinks; the recommended action gets a **glow pulse** (`glow`).
  - Disabled state: greyed.
  - Label sizing: labels auto-shrink, so "أعِد المشاهدة" ends up visibly smaller than its neighbours.
- **Card.** A picture (live 3D thumbnail) plus a letter disc (أ/ب/ج) and a label. Correct, wrong and dim states use colour changes and a popping ✓/✗ badge, which is clear feedback (`v2_quiz_answered.png`). Two problems:
  - The badge lands on top of the letter disc on text-only cards (890 vs 899-901; `v2_practice_answered.png`).
  - **UI bodies use lit materials** (800, 830, 878). Their brightness therefore depends on which way the station faces relative to the fixed sun (594). In the hall (frame yaw 300°) the card normals face away from the light: N·L ≈ -0.17, so the colour is about 0.61 of white. Because the Card label panel has `bg: null` (886), the label sits on that grey body, and **all quiz cards look muddy grey** (`v2_quiz.png`). Station 4 (yaw 180°) buttons are visibly darker than station 1's (`v2_st4_before.png` vs `v2_st1_before.png`). UI should be unlit or have a fixed light.

### 2.7 Station simulations (each animation is driven by one value t ∈ [0,1], which also feeds the thumbnails)

The station table (5.1 m in front, top 0.42 m above the ground) is a good diorama scale for VR.

1. **Earthquake (1303-1334).**
   - What it shows: a road with dashes, 3 Gulf-style houses (crenellated parapets and wind-tower *barjeel*), 3 trees and flowers. On play, the diorama content oscillates (it does not move the camera, which is good for comfort), houses rock, trees sway, 5 zig-zag cracks grow along their path, dust puffs rise and a rumble plays.
   - **Bug.** Cracks 3-5 are placed at y = 0.008 (1316-1318), below the platform top at y = 0.02 (1263), so they are **invisible**. Their dust puffs still appear "from nothing". The result after watching is two thin cracks on the road and nothing else (`v2_st1_after.png`, `v2_st1_after_down.png`).
   - Pedagogy: the shaking is clear while it plays, but the end state is weak. Nothing is displaced or fallen, and there is no fault offset.
2. **Volcano (1337-1371).**
   - What it shows: a lathe cone with a green skirt. On play, a lava pool fills the crater and pulses in colour, 3 lava ribbons grow down the flanks (tube `drawCount`), a soft glow appears, smoke puffs loop upward and a bubbling sound plays.
   - Pedagogy: clear and age-appropriate. There is no violent eruption and the words match: "خرجت الحمم من الفوهة وتدفّقت ببطء".
   - Look: the lava reads as orange ropes rather than molten rock, and there is no emissive bloom or heat glow on the ground (`v2_st2_after.png`).
3. **Weathering (1374-1421).**
   - What it shows: a big ico boulder. Dark crack tubes grow over its surface while it trembles. At t = 0.46 it is **swapped instantly** for 9 overlapping chunks, which then bounce out to rest beside it with grains around them (`v2_st3_mid.png` shows the chunks in a heap). A dashed white ring is meant to mark "the parts stay near the original spot".
   - **Bug.** The ring dashes are centred at y = 0.015 and 0.012 m thick (1401-1402), so their top is at 0.021, just 1 mm above the platform top at 0.02. The ring is effectively invisible (`v2_st3_after_down.png`).
   - Pedagogy: the result (broken in place) is clear, especially with the "التجوية = تفتُّت" chip. The **cause is never shown**: no water, heat/cold cycle, wind or roots. The rock simply cracks by itself.
4. **Erosion (1424-1507). The best station.**
   - What it shows: a hill with a Catmull-Rom stream over a soil bed, a scrolling water texture (flow speeds up while playing), a pool, 4 animated flow arrows, and 30 soil and rock bits that travel along the stream with lateral jitter. The source pile shrinks and the deposit pile grows. Signposts "من هنا" and "إلى هنا" appear and the chip reads "التعرية = انتقال".
   - It visualises transport explicitly, which is exactly the weathering vs erosion distinction the lesson needs.
   - **Bug.** From the viewing pad, the "من هنا" sign (at about 25° left, 1476) sits **behind the station info board** (1645), so only "إلى هنا" is visible, and Ali partly covers that one (`v2_st4_mid.png`, `v2_st4_after.png`).
5. **Human changes (1510-1570).**
   - What it shows: three grassy mounds with rocks, and a tracked excavator (boom, stick and bucket animate in a dig cycle) that drives across. The mounds flatten as the bucket passes, rocks disappear, and a road with edges and dashes extends behind it; an engine sound plays. Afterwards the before/after buttons and the easel ("قبل العمل" thumbnail) let the learner toggle the state (`v2_st5_after.png`, `v2_st5_compare_before.png`).
   - Pedagogy: clear. Weaknesses:
     - The road "grows" by scaling.
     - The excavator never carries soil.
     - Only one example (road building) is shown.
     - The "قارِن:" label is half hidden by the board pole, and the compare buttons float next to it.

### 2.8 Thumbnails (1755-1771)
- Each station is rendered offscreen at boot from a chosen camera and t (768×576), with Ali, the UI and the boards hidden. There is also a "humanBefore" render at t = 0. They are used on the practice and quiz cards and on the easel.
- **Strength.** The quiz pictures are exactly what the child saw, so they are consistent and readable (`v2_quiz.png`). Erosion is captured at t = 0.55, so the still conveys motion.
- **Weaknesses.**
  - No MSAA.
  - Only one picture per phenomenon, and the quiz reuses the same images, so it tests recognition, not transfer.
  - The quake thumbnail inherits the missing cracks.

### 2.9 Challenge hall (1717-1738)
- A lavender stage ring, 7 pastel pillars topped with sun balls, a torus arch, a coral "قاعة التحدّي" banner with trophies and 9 spinning stars. It is festive and readable (`v2_hall_intro.png`).
- The question board sits at 1.98 m, the progress pill above it, and the 3 cards in a row at about 1.1-1.2 m and 2.55 m away. The qStart and qNext buttons are on the waist lectern.
- The picture for "ما اسم هذا المظهر؟" is placed far left and angled (-2.1, 1.55, -2.7; 1732). On desktop it is half off-screen (`v2_quiz_q2.png`). The main stimulus should be centred.
- The cards are grey (see 2.6).
- The hall floor top (0.12) buries the viewer pad and Ali's feet.

### 2.10 Results and badge (1740-1752, 1926-1945)
- The result board is clear (big green "5 من 5", %, message and best score) and the 5 gold stars pop in sequence.
- The medallion "المستكشف الصغير" has teal and coral ribbons and bobs gently, and confetti falls (`v2_results.png`, `v2_results_badge.png`). This is a satisfying payoff.
- Missing:
  - there is no review of the wrong answers;
  - there is nothing to take away (certificate or teacher record);
  - the medallion is off to the left and overlaps a pillar.
- Numerals are inconsistent: Arabic-Indic in the hub labels (1682), Western in the progress and results lines (1901, 1933, 1942, 2131).

## 3. What is best in V2 and must be kept

1. **VR rig placement that keeps the user's head on the pad.** On every teleport the head's local XZ offset is subtracted, rotated by the target yaw (`enter`, 1787-1791).
2. **Snap turn that pivots around the head**, not the rig origin: 30° steps, with a cooldown and a click sound (`snapTurn`, 2052-2058; stick axis choice `axes[2]` vs `axes[0]` at 2038). Fix the cooldown being decremented once per controller.
3. **Controller ray + cursor + press feedback.**
   - The ray length is clamped to the hit distance.
   - Opacity rises on hover, and a hover tick sounds on a new target.
   - `selectstart` triggers press feedback and `select` triggers the click.
   - Code: 2014-2036, 2002-2003.

   **Add haptics.** V2 has none: pulse the gamepad's `hapticActuators[0]` on hover and on select.
4. **Gaze-dwell reticle** for pointer-less sessions: the ring scales with distance and stays a constant angular size, the dwell ring is filled by `drawCount`, the dwell is 1.6 s with a 1.2 s re-arm, and depthTest is off (1987-1990, 2040-2049).
5. **Waist-height angled control lectern** at (0, 0.74, -1.32) with tilt -0.85 rad, one per place, reused across stations (1688, 1737, 1797). It is easy to aim at, never blocks the diorama view, and works for seated and standing users.
6. **Comfort design.** No locomotion. Transitions use a warm-white fade (3.2/s). The earthquake shakes the *diorama*, never the camera (1324-1328). Session-safety guidance is shown on the intro (100-109).
7. **Pedagogical scaffolding:**
   - Prediction board: "ما اسم هذا المظهر؟ / شاهِد لتعرف", with the name revealed after watching (1650-1666).
   - Concept chips: "التجوية = تفتُّت" and "التعرية = انتقال" (1594, 1597, 1659-1662).
   - Targeted practice gates on the weathering and erosion pair (1604-1609).
   - Corrective feedback that explains the correct answer (1611-1615).
   - Glow pulse on the next recommended action.
8. **Erosion "من هنا / إلى هنا" signposts** (1470-1476), shrinking source and growing deposit piles (1495-1496), flowing water texture and flow arrows (1443-1447, 1500-1502). Fix the sign that hides behind the board.
9. **Weathering dashed "stays nearby" ring** (1401-1402, 1417) and the fragments coming to rest *next to* the origin. Raise the ring above the platform and make it thicker or animated.
10. **Before/after easel + قبل/بعد toggle** for human change (1700-1705, 1842-1853).
11. **Thumbnails rendered from the real stations** at chosen t values for practice, quiz and easel (1755-1771).
12. **Date-palm generator** `palmItems` (1026-1074): fronds, leaflets, dry fronds and date clusters. Port it to three.js as a merged BufferGeometry or instanced fronds, with fewer leaflets or LODs.
13. **Ali's outfit and cultural details**: thobe, vest, bush hat, Bahrain-flag backpack and sandals (1162-1207), plus the gesture blending (1239-1258).
14. **Gulf vernacular houses** with crenellated parapets and *barjeel* wind towers (1279-1294).
15. **Answer cards** with the letter disc, ✓/✗ pop badge and dim states (871-928); and **the HTML intro card**, which has device-specific "how to" guidance and graceful VR-unsupported messaging (2148-2150).
16. **The `drawCount` sweep technique** for growing cracks, lava, crack tubes and the dwell ring (tube `segIndexCount`, 472; `crackGeo`, 1266-1276).
17. **The synthesised SFX palette**, which has no asset weight (931-967), and the **test API** (2168-2175).

### 4.2 Design and product weaknesses
- **Passive learning.** The learner's only actions are pressing «شاهِد», «التالي» and answering. There is no hands-on manipulation: shaking the ground, pouring water to erode, freezing or heating the rock, or driving the excavator. In VR, agency is the main payoff, and V2 never uses the controllers except as pointers.
- **Weak cause and effect.** Weathering shows no agent. The earthquake leaves almost no lasting change. The volcano has no before/after comparison.
- **Assessment.** The 5 items have a fixed order and fixed option positions, and they reuse the exact learning images (recognition only). There is no review of mistakes and no retry of only the wrong items. The answer locks on the first trigger pull or a 1.6 s gaze, with no confirm step, which is risky for children pointing around.
- **Progress.**
  - The hub buttons show no done or practiced state.
  - There is no station map or progress bar.
  - The per-station state is lost on reload.
  - There is no student identity, teacher dashboard, CSV export or certificate (V1 has a dashboard, certificate and minimap).
- **VR-only gaps.**
  - The HTML HUD is hidden in VR (64), so there is no in-VR sound or voice toggle, help or "repeat what Ali said".
  - No recentring that turns the station toward the user's current head yaw on teleport (only the position is corrected).
  - Controllers are drawn as boxes.
  - No hand-tracking visuals, although `hand-tracking` is requested (1994).
  - No `visibilitychange` pause.
- **Narration.** Quest Browser normally has no Arabic TTS voice, so the experience is silent text for 8-9-year-olds, many of whom are still developing reading fluency. Recorded Arabic voice-over (or pre-generated audio) is essential.
- **Accessibility.** The 3D buttons cannot be reached with Tab or Enter (the canvas is focusable but has no keyboard activation). `prefers-reduced-motion` only affects the CSS. There is no text-size option.
- **Scale and art.** The character's head is small at the 3-3.5 m he stands from the pad. Hub buttons are 4.1 m away (about 3° tall), which makes them fiddly to aim at for children. The world is generic green with little Bahraini identity beyond the palms, houses and thobe.

## 5. Performance notes for Quest 2

Measured in headless Chromium through `__ali` (`tools/v2_shots2.mjs`; see `shots/v2_run2.log`):

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Scene nodes / meshes | 743 / 616 (616 unique geometries: **no sharing**; 332 materials) |
| Draw calls per view | **386 (hub), 396 (station 1)**, doubled in VR, so about 780-800 per frame |
| Triangles per view | **~589k visible** (605k total). The environment merged mesh alone is **447,884**. |
| Transparent draws | 49 per view, sorted every frame |
| Canvas textures | 40 (3.9 MPix) + 6 render targets 768×576 with depth: about 35-40 MB of VRAM with mips. Fine. |
| Boot | Intro visible after about 3.9 s headless; all geometry is generated synchronously on the main thread. |
| Headless FPS | About 5 on CPU SwiftShader (not representative; it only shows that `dt` is clamped to 0.05, so the simulation slows down instead of skipping). |

Analysis:
- **Triangles.** 589k triangles per eye, about 1.18 M per frame, plus MSAA on Adreno 650 at about 1832×1920 per eye is far above a comfortable WebXR budget (aim for ≤150-250k per eye). The cost is dominated by 16×12 spheres in canopies, bushes and clouds and by dense palm leaflets, most of which are behind the user or 20-40 m away. The fixes:
  - LOD or low-poly ico canopies;
  - instancing;
  - splitting the environment into sectors with frustum culling;
  - impostors for far trees.
- **CPU.** About 800 draws per frame, each with about 16 uniform calls, a CPU normal-matrix inversion (696) and a VAO bind. That is roughly 13k GL calls per frame from JS, which is likely to be CPU-bound on Quest Browser. In addition:
  - there is no frustum culling, so all 5 stations, the hall and the hub are drawn from everywhere (`collect`, 661-669);
  - there is no instancing;
  - there is no `OVR_multiview2`, so the scene is drawn twice;
  - `scene.updateWorld` recomputes all 743 matrices every frame;
  - every station's `tick` runs every frame even when unseen (2072);
  - per-frame allocations (`m4.create`, `v3.*` arrays, `allCtl()` arrays, `hexToRgb` string parsing in the volcano tick at 1365) create GC pressure, which shows up as hitches in VR.
- **Fill rate.** Back-face culling is disabled globally (679), and opaque meshes are not sorted front to back, so there is extra overdraw on a fill-limited mobile GPU. The fragment shader itself is cheap.
- **XR layer.**
  - `framebufferScaleFactor: 1.0` with `antialias: true` is fine.
  - Not used: `fixedFoveation` (Quest supports 0-1; about 0.5-1 saves a lot), `updateTargetFrameRate` (72 vs 90 Hz) and a `getNativeFramebufferScaleFactor` choice.
- **Audio.** Each `noise()` call allocates a new AudioBuffer of up to 12 s (947-948) on the main thread at play time. Fine on desktop, and a small hitch risk on Quest.
- **Good choices.** The single shader program means no program switches; the static environment is merged into one mesh; canvas textures are mipmapped and uploaded only when they change; thumbnails are rendered once at boot; and the simulation stays deterministic because each station is a pure function of t.

**Recommendation for the new build.** Use three.js (WebXR manager, multiview where available, instancing, frustum culling, BatchedMesh or merged static geometry, proper disposal). Show only the active station plus a low-LOD skyline. Use unlit or MSDF text for the UI. Set foveation to 1. Target ≤150 draw calls and ≤250k triangles per eye.
