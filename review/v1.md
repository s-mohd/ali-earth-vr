# Version 1 (Three.js r186) review: «مغامرة المستكشف علي في عالم الأرض المتغيرة»

Reviewer scope: `ref/v1_app.js` (all 10,373 lines read; the only parts skimmed are stock three.js add-ons: OrbitControls handlers 700–906 and BufferGeometryUtils `mergeGeometries`/`mergeVertices` 2785–3053), `ref/v1_style.css`, `ref/v1_body.html`, plus the running build.

All line numbers refer to `ref/v1_app.js` unless stated. Minified names used below: `q`=Mesh, `W`=Group, `H`=Vector3, `Io`=MeshStandardMaterial, `oi`=MeshBasicMaterial, `Po`=ShaderMaterial, `ki`=InstancedMesh, `Z()`=cached MeshStandardMaterial factory, `Bf()`=unlit canvas material, `Qf`=RoundedBoxGeometry, `ep`=3D button, `em`=answer card, `Bp`=board, `Yf`=speech bubble, `Zp`=Ali, `cm`=station controller, `Kh`=game controller, `gh`=HTML HUD.

**Verdict.** On desktop, V1 is a polished, warm, well-art-directed experience. All five simulations are choreographed, deterministic and scrubbable, and they teach the right contrasts, especially weathering versus erosion. The engineering is careful: a material cache, instancing, adaptive DPR, error and loading screens, a test API. It is not a VR game. It is a desktop game with a VR mode bolted on:
- Every meta-feature lives in HTML and disappears in the headset: map, stepper, help, sound, teacher, certificate, toasts, continue/new.
- The VR spawn point at the welcome gate is under the sea.
- VR floor heights are wrong after some transitions.
- Narration depends on an Arabic TTS voice that Quest Browser does not have.
- The render load is roughly 3–5× over a Quest 2 budget: about 250–530 draw calls and 350–460k triangles per mono frame before doubling for two eyes.

The content, the simulation architecture and the art are the parts to keep. The shell (HUD, VR placement, performance budget) has to be redesigned.

## 1) Feature inventory

### Flow
- **Boot and loading** (`ag` 10118–10350). Checks WebGL2 (`fd` 1–8). Shows a staged progress bar (`ng` 10083–10090) with Arabic step labels: font, scene, island, gate, Ali, picture rendering, stations, hall, map, effects. It waits for the Cairo font (`rg` 10091–10102, 6 s timeout, toast on fallback). It precompiles shaders (`renderer.compileAsync` 10304). The error screen has a retry button (`$h` 10070–10076). Global `error` and `unhandledrejection` handlers sit at 10351–10373, and a `webglcontextlost` handler at 10149–10155 says progress will resume.
- **Welcome / hub at the island gate** (`arriveWelcome` 9389–9404, gate model `Up` 4217–4321):
  - An HTML speech card shows an offscreen-rendered Ali portrait (`hud.showWelcome` 8178–8193).
  - The menu offers «ابدأ المغامرة / تابع المغامرة», «طريقة اللعب», sound and «وضع VR».
  - A "new adventure from scratch" link appears once progress exists (8014–8031).
  - Ali waves. TTS reads the welcome only once audio has been unlocked.
- **Five sequential stations** around a ring path (`tp` zones 2766–2775; stations `cm` 5266–5646, defs `Ip` 3586–3651).
  - Ali walks to station 1 with the camera following him (`walkTo` 9278–9325). Later sequential moves also walk; non-sequential moves fade (`travel` 9251–9277).
  - Each station runs intro, «شاهد…», play, explanation board, optional practice, then «المحطة التالية» (`setPhase` 5608–5626).
  - Stations unlock in order (`ph` 7941–7946, `goTo` 9798–9831).
- **Practice questions**: two of them, both 2-option, at weathering and erosion only (`_h` 8555–8580).
  - Practice starts automatically 2.6 s after the animation (`onPlayComplete` 9480–9496).
  - A wrong answer shakes the card and Ali encourages; the card re-enables after 1.4 s.
  - A right answer earns praise and marks the station done (9517–9549).
- **Challenge gate, then challenge hall** (`Km` 7113–7370; `startChallenge` 9569–9625): Ali walks into a circular colonnade with bunting. A question board and three answer cards (أ/ب/ج) appear, some with images.
  - **Picture quiz**: five questions (`rm` 5123–5212), and each answer locks once (`yh` 8584–8596).
  - Feedback: correct turns the card green with a check, wrong turns it orange with a cross, and the right card is revealed after 750 ms (`reveal` 7346–7356). Ali reacts with randomized phrases (9654–9687).
  - Progress dots sit on the board (7292–7298).
- **Results hall** (`Ym` 7391–7683; `arriveResults` 9720–9746):
  - A score board shows score and percent with pass/fail text. Five extruded stars pop in one by one with tones; balloons float.
  - On a pass: 160 instanced confetti pieces, Ali celebrates, and the certificate appears on a 3D easel with a «تنزيل الشهادة» button.
  - Retry, review and home buttons. The pass mark is 80% (`nm` 5122, `Sh` 8603–8616).
- **Resume**: progress (stations done, practice done and tries, location, in-flight challenge, last result) is saved to **sessionStorage** (`ch/lh/uh` 7907–7933). «تابع المغامرة» resumes at the saved location (`start` 9405–9427).

### Ali lip-sync and poses
- `talk(seconds)` sets a mouth flap for `0.6 + 0.06·chars` seconds, capped at 6 (9167). The mouth alternates open and smile at 16 rad/s (4760–4762). It is a timer, not driven by TTS boundary events.

### XR handling
- `vf` (1834–1934). The session is `immersive-vr` with optional features `local-floor` and `bounded-floor` (1900–1902).
- Each of two controllers gets an orange ray line, a tip sphere and a blue cylinder grip (1881–1896). Rays hover the 3D UI and `select` clicks (1246–1274).
- There is a camera-attached fade sphere (1850–1862, 1927–1933). On travel the player is teleported with a fade (`placeVR` 9332–9339, `placeAt` 1921–1926).
- On entering VR the HTML HUD is hidden and orbit controls are disabled (`onXRChange` 9928–9937). A 3D «ابدأ المغامرة» button shows at the gate (10238–10247).
- A pre-entry modal gives a safety/age notice (`openVRInfo` 8453–8492).
- Missing: snap turn, teleport, hand-tracking, haptics, and any in-VR menu.

### Desktop controls
- Limited OrbitControls (`Zd` 979–1121) with damped rotate and zoom, no pan, polar limits of 0.32–1.38 rad, and per-view azimuth, distance and target-radius limits.
- WASD and arrows nudge the camera (964–973, 1102–1114).
- Keys (10004–10030): `1/2/3` and `١/٢/٣` answer, `R` resets the view, `M` opens the map, `H`/`?` opens help, `Enter`/`Space` triggers the primary action.
- Click versus drag is discriminated at 7 px / 1.2 s (1122–1123, 1163–1181).
- FOV keeps the horizontal framing on narrow screens (`ig` 10103–10117).

### Accessibility
- RTL document (10061) and `aria-live` HUD and toasts (body.html).
- `aria-label`s on icon buttons, `aria-current` on the stepper, and modal focus trap and restore (8355–8373).
- The canvas has an `aria-label` (21–22).
- `prefers-reduced-motion` applies to CSS only. Camera walks, shakes and confetti are not reduced.
- Correct and wrong are signalled with icons as well as colour (check/cross badges 5001–5003).

### Performance helpers
- Material cache `Z` (2315–2325), procedural geometry cache `lp` (3054–3057), InstancedMesh vegetation (3880–3964).
- Adaptive pixel ratio in 2D only (`md` 27–51): it steps from 1.75 down to 0.85 when the frame time exceeds 26 ms and never steps back up.
- On desktop, rendering is throttled to every fourth frame while a modal is open (10313–10319).

### Test hooks
- `?test` exposes `window.__ali` (10342–10349) and `?speed=N` sets the time scale (10126). `snapshot()` returns the full game state (10042–10059).

## 2) Per-component quality assessment

### World and island (`zp` 3654–4051)
- **Layout**: a round island. A sand path ring at r=36 (`buildPaths` 3831–3861) links eight zones placed by angle (gate 90°, then the stations at 132/180/228/276/324°, the challenge at 372°, results at 420°; `tp` 2766–2775).
  - `heightAt` (3673–3689) is 3-octave value noise plus a central hill. Each zone is flattened with a smoothstep disc, and so are the ring path and the gate spur. The shore falls off from r=57 to 66.
  - The shore radius is found by bisection-like scanning (3743–3747).
- **Terrain**: one 152×152 plane with **168×168 segments (56,448 triangles)** (3749). It is vertex-coloured with grass noise, per-zone ground tints (`zoneGroundWeight` 3690–3700), sand and wet-sand bands, and a shallow-water tint. It looks good, but it is over-tessellated for such gentle relief.
- **Sky**: a 500 m gradient sphere shader with a sun disc and glow (`Mp` 3455–3490). It is cheap and pretty.
- **Sea**: a 520 m circle shader (`Np` 3491–3545). Depth colour depends on distance from shore. There are animated sparkle bands and **two animated foam rings with angular wobble** at the shoreline, and it supports fog. It is a very nice, cheap effect.
- **Background**: 20 noise-blob mountains, each a separate mesh with its own draw call (3546–3569), and 16 merged-geometry clouds (3570–3585).
- **Vegetation**: palms, three round trees, pines, bushes, 400 grass tufts, 300 flowers and two rock sizes. All are scattered with exclusion zones (`isFree` 3862–3879) into **one InstancedMesh per kind** (1328 instances). Each geometry is a merged, vertex-coloured mesh built procedurally.
  - The look is consistent and charming (`shots/v1_hub.png`).
  - Weakness: each InstancedMesh spans the whole island, so frustum culling never rejects it. All 1328 instances (about 137k triangles) are drawn every frame, in both the main and the shadow pass (see §5).
- **Centerpiece and gate**: a gazebo with a CPU-animated flag (3982–4019, 4027–4033). The gate (`Up`) has pillars, a bush arch, a flower ring, a title board and two waving pennants, which are CPU vertex-animated every frame forever (4301–4311).
- **Lighting**: a hemisphere light (1.55), a 2048² PCF sun shadow following the current focus point (`setShadowFocus` 3738–3742), and ACES tone mapping. The volcano also adds a **PointLight that is always in the scene** (6167–6169). Even at intensity 0 it adds a point-light loop to every lit shader in the world.
- Assessment: the art direction is high quality and coherent. The `X` palette (1947–2005) is a ready-made design token set. The cost is too high for Quest, and nothing is culled per zone.

### Ali: character, poses, lip-sync (`Zp` 4389–4853; materials `Yp` 4350–4378)
- **Build**: a procedural Bahraini boy.
  - Body: a lathe-geometry thobe with a collar placket and buttons, sandals, and segmented arms (shoulder, elbow and wrist pivots) with a hand, a thumb pivot and a hidden index finger for pointing.
  - Head: a big chibi head, hair cap, fringe tufts, layered eyes (white, iris, pupil, two highlights), capsule brows, nose, blush discs, three mouth shapes (smile torus, open mouth with tongue, "o"), a lathe ghutra crown and drape with a front band, and two torus agal rings.
  - **Inverted-hull outlines** (`qp` 4338–4349) on 12 parts. The result is **64 meshes and 20.4k triangles**, scaled ×1.35 (4622).
- **Poses** (`targetPose` 4648–4765): idle (breathing and head sway), wave, point (aims the right arm at a world target with `aimDirection` 4770–4775 and turns head and body toward it), think (hand to chin, eyes up), thumbsUp (bounce), encourage, celebrate (both arms up, bounce) and walk (arm swing, foot slide, bob).
  - Blending is a per-joint quaternion slerp with exponential smoothing (`applyPose` 4776–4838), plus scalar "extras" (thumb, bounce, happy-eye squint, brow raise, look-up).
  - Blink timer (4842–4845) and yaw smoothing toward a target or the camera (4846, 10033–10038).
- **Lip-sync**: timer-based flapping (4760–4762). It is not tied to audio, but it still shows that Ali is speaking when TTS is missing, which is the normal case on Quest.
- Assessment: **the best asset in V1.** It is expressive, culturally right and readable at a distance (`shots/v1_st1_after.png`, `v1_results.png`). Its weaknesses are the draw-call count (64 meshes, so about 128 per frame in stereo) and the feet, which slide rather than step.

### Speech bubble (`Yf` 2395–2506)
- A single plane whose CanvasTexture is rebuilt on every `say()`, with the old texture disposed (2466–2469). It word-wraps Arabic correctly, with RTL and RLE/PDF embedding (2016–2018, 2051–2064). It has an optional speaker icon and a tail that can point left, right or centre.
- It is placed at Ali's head +0.55 up, with a ±0.35 side offset, and then **moved 0.6 m toward the camera** (2494–2505). It copies the **full camera quaternion**, so in VR it rolls with head tilt.
- Problems:
  - (a) It is large (up to 6.2 units wide, 2420) and sits between the camera and the phenomenon, so **it covers the rock, the stream and the excavator** in `v1_st3_after.png`, `v1_st4_after.png` and `v1_st5_after.png`.
  - (b) `depthTest:true` means scene geometry can clip it.
  - (c) It never scales with distance in VR.

### 3D buttons, cards and boards
- **Buttons `ep`** (2600–2765): attractive and tactile (`shots/v1_st5_toggle_before.png`). Problems:
  - Each button allocates **two new MeshStandardMaterials** (body and base, 2638–2650) plus a canvas texture.
  - Each button ticks every frame even when hidden: the `mf` subscription at 2668 returns early only when `!visible`.
  - Station buttons are placed per station in layout space (`om`/`sm` 5215–5265) and sometimes end up behind Ali (station 1) or off-frame (the volcano's `buttons:[-5.4,1.1,5.6]`, 5240; see `v1_st2_after.png`, where none of the 3D buttons is on screen).
- **Cards `em`** (4863–5055): clear state language (`$p` colours 4855–4862, check/cross badges, dimming of the others, shake on wrong, lift on hover). The reveal pulse (5051) is nice. Images come from the offscreen thumbnails, so the quiz pictures match the simulations exactly (`v1_quiz.png`).
- **Boards `Bp`** (4052–4189):
  - Typography is good: the kicker in mint, the title at 92–118 px with an orange underline, the body at 70 px and a footnote at 50 px.
  - The 200 px/unit resolution gives 1320×780 canvases, which are sharp on desktop. At 11 m in VR the body text is about 0.35 m tall, roughly 1.8°, which is readable.
  - The erosion **compare board** (5434–5490) puts the weathering and erosion images side by side. It is excellent pedagogically, but the left label is clipped at the canvas edge ("التعرية = انتقال الفتا…", `shots/v1_zoom_erosion_board.png`; the label's `maxWidth r+40` overflows, 5475–5480).

### Station simulations

All five follow one contract, which is V1's best architectural idea:
- `{ group, duration, cues[], focus, pictureView, pictureTime, applyAt(t), update(dt,t) }`.
- `applyAt(t)` is a **pure function of time**, so it can be replayed, scrubbed, frozen at the final state (`showFinal` 5571–5576), toggled before/after (`toggleBeforeAfter` 5580–5589) and rendered as a thumbnail at `pictureTime` (7765).
- The station controller drives it (`cm.tick` 5590–5607), fires the sound cues and completes the station.

1. **Earthquake** (`_m` 5774–5981, 4.9 s). Shown: a two-lane road with dashes, a house, a lamp post, a fence, trees, rocks and a dust sprite pool (12).
   - The ground shakes with an envelope of `sin(πt)^0.8` over 0.5–3.7 s. Each "shaker" has its own motion type: the house slides and rocks, trees sway, rocks hop, the road jitters and the fence slides (5927–5949).
   - "Shake marks" (motion-line arcs) pulse around the house (5953–5962).
   - **The crack reveal**: a zig-zag crack ribbon and a lighter rim ribbon are built with `lm()` (5841–5848). Their `aReveal` attribute equals the normalized distance from the road centre, so the crack **grows outward from the road in both directions** as `uReveal` goes 0→1 (5963–5965). Three branch cracks reveal along their length on staggered timers (5966–5969).
   - The left road slab drops 7 cm and tilts (5950–5952). Dust puffs rise along the crack (5970–5978). Cues: rumble at 0.45 s, crack at 1.35 s and 2.3 s.
   - Clarity: very good, with an unmistakable crack across the road (`v1_st1_after.png`). Weak point: the house shows no damage afterwards, so the "after" image is only the crack.
2. **Volcano** (`Tm` 6052–6233, 9 s). A lathe cone (`vm` profile 5982–5995) with noise-displaced flanks (`Sm` 6036–6040) and vertex colours running green → brown → dark crater.
   - Lava pool: a radial lava-shader disc rises in the crater and widens (6201–6205).
   - **Lava flow**: a ribbon of 61 cross-sections draped 7–8 cm above the cone. The `xm` shader (5998–6035) discards beyond the `uProgress` front with a wobbly edge and draws flowing hot bands, with a brighter leading edge.
   - The base pool grows at the end (6210–6212). A point light glows (6214) and 16 smoke sprites rise in a loop (6216–6232).
   - Clarity: good. The lava visibly comes out of the crater and runs down (`v1_st2_after.png`).
   - Problems: (a) the **cone pokes through the lava ribbon** in several places (`v1_zoom_volcano.png`) because the 0.07–0.08 m offset is smaller than the noise displacement; (b) the smoke is near-invisible against the pale sky, so "مواد بركانية" other than lava is not really shown; (c) the volcano camera framing pushes the 3D buttons off-screen.
3. **Weathering** (`Om` 6236–6478, 6.6 s, tagged «محاكاة مسرّعة» while it plays). A big squashed noise rock (detail 4) carries six embedded chunks, placed by raycasting the rock surface (6277–6286).
   - **Cracks are TubeGeometry outlines drawn with a growing `setDrawRange`** (6345–6351, 6425–6429). Chunks wobble, then pop off, tumble and land 2.75–3.45 m away, staying near the rock (6430–6455). 26 instanced pebbles scatter (6456–6473), and the rock shrinks by 3.5% (6474–6475).
   - Clarity: the key idea, that **fragments stay close to the parent rock**, is shown well and is the explicit contrast with erosion (`v1_practice_weathering.png`).
   - Problems: (a) no weathering *agent* is shown (water, heat, roots); (b) the rock stays intact with no holes where chunks left; (c) the bubble hides the rock in the "after" frame.
4. **Erosion** (`Fm` 6536–6750, 8.8 s). A 20×16 m mini-landscape (110×88 grid, 19k triangles): a hillside with a carved channel (`Nm` 6485–6497), a soil and stone pile at the top, and a pond basin at the bottom.
   - Water: a ribbon with the `Pm` shader (6498–6535) reveals downhill. Its stripes stream along the flow and the edges are translucent. The pond grows (6702–6705).
   - **68 instanced particles** (34 soil and 34 stone) travel along the CatmullRom stream curve with lane offsets and bouncing, then fan out into a deposit at the pond (6710–6733).
   - Four extruded orange arrows pulse along the tangent (6736–6748).
   - Clarity: **the best station.** Source, transport and deposit are all visible, and the board then shows the weathering-versus-erosion comparison (`v1_st4_after.png`). Problem: the bubble overlaps the middle of the stream.
5. **Human changes** (`Wm` 6844–7111, 13.2 s). A building site with three dirt mounds, cones, barriers, palms and an articulated excavator (`Um` 6762–6843): tracks, turret, boom, arm, bucket and a soil lump.
   - The excavator drives to each mound and digs (boom, arm and bucket curl). It swings 1.45 rad, dumps (6 falling soil lumps, a pile grows) and returns. Then the ground smooths (`rough`→0), the machine drives away and **the road paves in left to right** through the same reveal shader (`aReveal` from x, 6896–6906, 7100–7101). The curbs scale in and the lane dashes pop in sequence (7102–7107).
   - A "قبل العمل / بعد العمل" tag sits above, and there is a «قبل / بعد» toggle (`v1_st5_toggle_before.png`).
   - Clarity: very good, with a narrative order of dig, move soil, flatten, build road.
   - Cost: the ground is a 97×51 grid **re-evaluated on the CPU, with `computeVertexNormals`, every frame** while playing or toggling (`rebuildGround` 6991–7010, called from `applyAt` 7039).

### Thumbnails (`nh` 7734–7809)
- For each station it builds a **second copy** of the model, places it on a ground disc in its own scene with its own lights (`eh` 7717–7732), applies `pictureTime` and renders at 640×460 into a canvas. The images feed the quiz cards, the practice cards and the compare board.
- Ali's portrait is rendered the same way at 520×620 with alpha (7780–7802) for the welcome card and the certificate.
- Fallback: a labelled gradient placeholder (`Lf` 2298–2314) plus a toast.
- **Technique: excellent** (the visuals stay consistent with no image assets). **Implementation: expensive.**

## 4) Bugs, fragile code and UX issues

### 4a. VR blockers and VR-unreachable features (Quest 2)

**Critical: VR spawn at the welcome is under the sea.** `placeVR` puts the player at `target.lerp(pos, 0.72)` (9333). For the welcome view, `pos` is gate-local (0.6, 4.9, 21.5) at r≈73 (10267–10268), outside the island. The stand point resolves to **(0.6, −2.8, 67.5)**: on the sea floor, 2.25 m below the water plane at y=−0.55 (measured, `review/v1_stats.json` → `welcome:vr`; render `shots/v1_vr_welcome.png`). Entering VR from the start screen, which is the normal path, places the child in a pit under the sea surface. The sea is a single-sided plane, so it is invisible from below.

**High: wrong VR floor height after some transitions.** `travel()` calls `placeVR(i)` (9261) before `arrive(e)` updates `this.location` (9359). The `+0.3` floor offset for the challenge and results platforms (9335–9337) is therefore computed from the **previous** location. Reproduced in `tools/v1_bug_probe.mjs`:
- Challenge to station: player y=0.30, floating 0.3 m above the station ground.
- Gate to challenge: player y=0.00, with feet 0.3 m inside the hall floor.

**High: HTML HUD hidden in VR with no 3D replacement** (`onXRChange` → `hud.setHidden(true)` 9930). Unreachable in the headset:
- Stepper and map navigation (8246–8316, 9832–9881). After «مراجعة المحطات» the toast tells the child to use the stepper or map (9795), and neither exists in VR.
- Help (8375–8416), sound on/off (8067–8072), the teacher dashboard, and the HTML listen button. The bubble 🔊 exists only when a TTS voice exists (2425, 2480).
- «ابدأ مغامرة جديدة من الصفر» and the continue/new choice (8014–8023). In VR only the 3D start button exists (10238–10247).
- **Toasts** for station completion, locked stations and "watch first" (9501–9505, 9561, 9805, 9815) are invisible, so VR feedback for locks is silent.
- **Certificate**: the 3D «تنزيل الشهادة» (7506–7515) calls `openCertificate` (9758), which opens an **invisible HTML modal** with a text input. Nothing happens in the headset, and the modal is waiting when the child exits VR.
- The VR info and safety modal can only be read before entering.

**High: no 3D welcome text in VR.** When VR starts at the welcome, `onXRChange` only shows the 3D start button (9933–9934). On desktop the welcome bubble is hidden by design (9399–9402), so the child sees no welcome speech in VR unless they leave and return.

**High: narration silent on Quest.**
- All voice comes from `speechSynthesis` with an Arabic voice (1757–1776). Quest Browser normally exposes no Arabic voice (headless Chromium reported `[]` as well), so `available=false`: no narration and no speaker icon.
- The prepared fallback message `Q.noArabicVoice` (5113) is never used.
- Grade-3 readers in VR get text only, at 5–11 m.

**Medium: VR ergonomics.**
- There is no teleport, snap turn or thumbstick locomotion. The player is only teleported per location, and the child must physically turn to see off-axis UI (for example the volcano buttons at local [-5.4, 1.1, 5.6], 5240).
- Measured distances from the VR stand point (`review/v1_stats.json`):
  - Station 1 buttons: 4.7–5.2 m, at 0.8 m height.
  - Challenge cards: **10.1–10.8 m**.
  - Results download: **10.9 m**, occluded by the retry button (`v1_vr_results.png`).
  - Too far for comfortable laser pointing by a child, and the quiz feels remote.
- The bubble copies the head roll (2496–2497).
- No haptics on hover or select (1269–1273).
- **`hand-tracking` is not requested** (1901), so a child without controllers cannot interact.
- No gaze or dwell fallback.
- Picking ignores occluders: `pick` intersects only the candidates (1214), so buttons behind boards or Ali can still be clicked.

**Medium: XR session error path.** If `setSession` throws (for example `local-floor` is not granted), the catch (1914–1916) returns `false` without calling `session.end()`, which leaves an orphaned session.

### 4b. Gameplay and logic bugs
- **Critical (pedagogical): a stale "reveal" timer highlights the wrong answer on the next question.**
  - `Km.reveal` schedules `setTimeout(() => cards[t].setState('reveal'), 750)` (7355) and never cancels it.
  - After a wrong answer the **HUD primary «السؤال التالي» is enabled immediately** (`refreshHUD` called at 9686, and `enabled: !t && !!e` at 9985). Enter or Space also triggers it (10025–10027).
  - Moving on within about 430 ms, the 750 ms reveal minus the 320 ms transition (9695), lets the old timer paint the new question's card at the old correct index green with a check.
  - Reproduced: Q2 shows «التجوية» as correct (`shots/v1_bug_stale_reveal.png`; probe output `q2CardStates:["idle","reveal","idle"]`). The real answer is «البركان». The highlighted card is still clickable and is wrong.
  - The 3D next button only appears after 900 ms (9675–9685), so desktop and keyboard users are the ones at risk.
- `hidePractice` hides the group with an uncancelled 300 ms `setTimeout` (5553–5555). If practice is shown again within 300 ms (replay → complete at high speed) it gets hidden.
- **Practice auto-starts 2.6 s after the animation** (9490–9493) and moves the camera away from the explanation board before the child has read it.
- **Duplicate controls on desktop**: the 3D station buttons and the HTML bottom bar perform the same actions with different labels and positions (`v1_st1_before.png`: two «شاهد الزلزال» buttons). This is confusing for children.
- **The speech bubble occludes the phenomenon** at weathering, erosion and human (§2). Ali also stands in front of the 3D buttons at station 1 (`v1_st1_after.png`).
- Visual bugs:
  - Certificate title underline overlaps the wrapped title (8839–8847).
  - Erosion compare-board label clipped (5475–5480).
  - Lava ribbon poke-through (6121–6157).
  - A palm frond pierces the volcano board (green sliver on «ماذا سيحدث؟», `v1_st2_before.png`).
- **Progress lives in sessionStorage** (7920, 7929). It is lost when the tab or the Quest Browser closes, so "تابع المغامرة" only works within one browser session. Teacher attempts are in localStorage (8619).
- `setVRSupported` toggles a `.dim` class that has no CSS rule (8225; style.css has no `.dim`), so it is a no-op.
- The teacher dashboard is not protected and is one click from the student HUD (8097–8108).
- Print (`zh` 8910–8924) uses a `window.open` popup, which Quest Browser and many school browsers block. The toast tells the user to allow popups.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` covers CSS only. Camera walk paths (9278–9325), shakes and confetti ignore it.
- Observed in headless only: modal CSS keyframe animations start at opacity 0 (`fadeIn`/`pop` in style.css). With a stalled animation timeline (SwiftShader) the map modal stayed fully transparent until the animations were force-finished (`tools/v1_map_debug.mjs`). In a real browser this is likely fine, but a modal that depends on the animation finishing to become visible is fragile.

### 4c. Fragile code
- Ordering-dependent state (`location` updated after placement, 9258–9276 vs 9359) and many uncancelled `setTimeout`s tied to game state (7355, 5553, 9183, 9534, 9543, 9676, 9736). Async UI should run on the game clock (`gf`/`_f`) and be cancellable per location.
- Per-frame allocations in hot paths (`follow` 2496–2503, `rig.update` 1106–1108, `applyAt` matrices 6456–6459, 6706–6709) cause GC churn. On Quest, GC pauses show up as dropped frames.
- Every station's `model.update` runs every frame even off-screen (5591). Smoke sprites and arrows keep animating far away.
- The adaptive pixel ratio only goes down, and it is disabled in XR (27–51). There is no dynamic resolution or foveation control in VR; the three.js defaults are foveation 1.0 and framebufferScale 1.0.

**Where the load comes from**
- **The island itself.**
  - Terrain: 56k triangles, one draw.
  - Island-wide InstancedMeshes: 14 draws, about **137k triangles, never frustum-culled** because each instanced mesh's bounds cover the whole island. Breakdown: round trees 73×756 tris, palms 31×848, bushes 80×240, tufts 398×40, flowers 296×36, pebbles 260×20, rocks 42×80.
  - Ali: 64 meshes, 20k triangles.
  - The shadow pass re-draws about 110 casters (around 400 meshes have `castShadow=true`, because `Vf` sets it on everything; e.g. 3360, 4017, 5924).
- **Per-zone mesh counts** (`perf.json` breakdown): human 110, hall 117, earthquake 72, results 62, weathering 49, erosion 39, volcano 28.
- **Materials**: **174 `MeshStandardMaterial`s** (PBR), 61 MeshBasic, 28 SpriteMaterial (one cloned per smoke or dust sprite, 3449–3452), 7 ShaderMaterial, compiled into 31 programs.
  - Lighting is hemisphere + directional (2048² **PCF** shadow, `shadowMap.type=1`) + an always-present **PointLight** (6167), with ACES tone mapping (`toneMapping=4`).
  - Every lit fragment does PBR maths for 1 directional + 1 point light plus a shadow lookup. This is the most expensive fragment path on the Quest 2 Adreno 650.
- **Textures**: **56 canvas textures, 18.1 MP**, about **92 MB of GPU memory with mipmaps** (RGBA8).
  - All NPOT (e.g. question board 2200×670, boards 1320×780, cards 665×760), with anisotropy up to 8 (`wf` 2009–2012, 10136).
  - Most are `transparent:true` (`Bf` 2329–2331), so they are sorted and blended every frame.
- **CPU per frame**:
  - The human ground rebuild while playing (4,947 verts with noise plus `computeVertexNormals`, 6991–7010).
  - CPU vertex animation of 3 flags (4027–4033, 4301–4311).
  - Confetti matrices (7667–7681) and erosion and weathering instance matrices.
  - All button and card tickers (`hf` 1806–1809) and per-frame allocations.
- **Boot**:
  - 3 extra WebGL contexts (7705, 7781, 7813), each with full program compiles and geometry uploads.
  - 5 duplicate station graphs.
  - A top-down map render of the full scene at 900².
  - About 230 KB of fonts embedded twice, as woff2 **and** woff (7 faces, v1.html).
  - Measured 10.6 s to interactive in SwiftShader. On Quest expect several seconds plus shader compilation.

### Estimated Quest 2 stereo cost
- With three.js r186's `WebGLRenderer`, WebXR has **no multiview**: multiview exists only in the WebGPU renderer's `common/XRManager`. The main pass is issued per eye.
- At a station in VR: about 2×150–260 main calls + about 110 shadow calls = **410–630 draw calls and about 570–850k triangles per frame**.
- At the welcome view: about 950 calls and 790k triangles.
- Each eye renders about 1832×1920 with 4× MSAA (`antialias:true`, 10) and full PBR, against a 13.9 ms budget at 72 Hz.
- A practical WebXR budget on Quest 2 is about 100–150 draw calls and about 100–200k triangles with simple shading. **V1 is 3–5× over budget** and would run at roughly 30–45 fps with reprojection judder, worse during the human-station CPU rebuild.

### Recommendations for the new build
1. **Zone streaming**: render only the current zone, a low-poly island backdrop and the sky and sea. Hide the other zones (`zone.frame.visible=false`). Split vegetation instancing into **per-zone or per-sector InstancedMeshes** so frustum culling works.
2. **Lighting and shading**: drop the PointLight and use an emissive crater and lava. Replace MeshStandard with Lambert or toon shading on vertex colours, with AO baked into the vertex colours. Keep ACES, or bake a LUT, only if fill-rate allows.
3. **Shadows**: shadow only Ali and the active machine, with a small ±8 m shadow camera at 1024², or use blob shadows. Set `shadowMap.autoUpdate=false` outside animations. Do not let static props or vegetation cast.
4. **Geometry**: terrain at about 64² (or a precomputed flat zone plus a skirt). Merge Ali to about 10 meshes and merge each station's static props (`mergeGeometries` is already bundled, 2785). Merge the bunting, balloons and barriers.
5. **Textures**: use a few shared atlases at power-of-two sizes. Make canvas faces opaque with alphaTest. Cap anisotropy at 2–4. Destroy the thumbnail canvases after upload.
6. **XR**: set foveation and framebuffer scale explicitly (`renderer.xr.setFoveation(1)`, scale 0.9–1.0). Request `hand-tracking`. Add a dynamic-resolution step-down for VR, which the current adaptive DPR does not do.
7. **Boot**: render the thumbnails and the map into render targets on the main renderer. Ship only woff2 fonts.
8. **CPU**: run human-station terrain morphing in the vertex shader or as precomputed keyframe blends. Run tickers and timeouts on the game clock and skip work for hidden zones.
